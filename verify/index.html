A certificate is not a certification

The pathway issues two different things, and the difference is the whole point of the standard.

The Bulletproof Automation Foundations Certificate records completion of training. It attests that someone attended and completed the programme and submitted a capstone project. It is not an assessment of professional competence, and it makes no independent competency claim.

The Bulletproof Certified Automation Builder (BCAB) is a professional certification. It is assessed across four components against a published standard, it can be failed, and it is valid for 24 months. Public conferral of BCAB opens on 1 November 2026, when the first assessment window begins.

Checking a claim you have been handed

Our certificates carry a QR code. It is a convenience, not a proof — and it is worth being plain about why.

A QR code on a printed certificate can point anywhere. Whoever printed the document chose where it goes. So if it matters, do not scan it: type training.bulletproofautomations.com/verify into your browser yourself and enter the credential ID printed alongside it. A forged certificate can imitate this page; what it cannot do is make our address return its record.

Every certificate we issue prints the ID and the address in plain text next to the code, precisely so you never have to trust the code.
