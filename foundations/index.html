The eight classes

Every class ends with something exported, screenshotted and written up. By week eight that is a portfolio, not a certificate you have to be taken at your word about.

  1. Class 1

    Getting started with n8n

    You build your first workflow: a manual trigger, a step that creates a simple profile, and a step that formats a confirmation message.

    After it, you can open n8n, explain what you are looking at, build and run something, and export proof of it.

    Proof: workflow JSON, screenshots, and notes explaining what it does

  2. Class 2

    Triggers and data

    An intake flow that captures name, email, experience level and goal, normalises the fields, then builds a summary with expressions.

    After it, you can tell what starts a workflow, read the data it delivers, and reference that data correctly further down.

    Proof: export, screenshots of the trigger output and the expression-built summary, notes

  3. Class 3

    Connecting apps

    You take the intake data and append it to a Google Sheet, then read the rows back to confirm it actually landed.

    After it, you can connect a real application with a stored credential and verify the result rather than assuming it.

    Proof: export, a screenshot of the appended row, a sanitised canvas screenshot, notes

  4. Class 4

    Working with APIs

    An external lookup: call a public API, read the status code and the JSON, pull values out of nested data, and combine them with your intake data.

    After it, you can make a request, understand what came back, and get the part you need out of it.

    Proof: export, screenshots of the response and the extracted fields, notes

  5. Class 5

    Logic and branching

    Routing: an IF that sends beginners and non-beginners different ways, extended to a three-way Switch on what the person wants to build.

    After it, you can design decision paths — and you treat an untested branch as broken until you have run it.

    Proof: export, screenshots proving both branches and two Switch routes ran, each rule written in plain English

  6. Class 6

    Error handling

    You break the Class 4 lookup on purpose, then make it survive: retries, an error branch, and a separate handler workflow that catches failures.

    After it, you can say what happens when a workflow fails — because you have watched yours fail and handled it.

    Proof: two exports, before and after screenshots of the failure, notes on what can break

  7. Class 7

    AI-powered automation

    An AI step inside a controlled workflow: send an application to a free model, get back a short summary and a category as structured JSON.

    After it, you can add AI where it genuinely helps, ask for output you can rely on, and check what comes back.

    Proof: export, screenshots of the prompt and two runs showing variation, notes

  8. Class 8

    Capstone

    One pipeline that uses the lot: an intake trigger, data shaping, an external lookup or app logging, an AI summary, and error handling that holds.

    After it, you have a complete beginner automation that survives failure and that you can explain in ninety seconds.

    Proof: final export plus the error handler, screenshots of two test cases including one handled failure, and a written explanation

How the certificate is awarded

This is a certificate of completion, not a professional certification. It records that you completed the training and can build at a foundational level. It makes no independent competency claim beyond readiness to progress, and it says so on the certificate itself.

Three requirements, all of them mandatory:

  1. Participation — you meet the published attendance minimum.
  2. Portfolio evidence — you submit the weekly artifacts for each class: the workflow export, screenshots, and your notes.
  3. Capstone — you complete it to the published specification and explain it satisfactorily in a walkthrough.

There is no exam. There is also no direct-entry route: a certificate of completion cannot be awarded for training that was not completed, so there is no way to buy or test out of this stage.

Where this sits in the pathway

Foundations is Stage 1. It is the taught route into Stage 2, the Bulletproof Certified Automation Builder certification — which, unlike this, is assessed and can be not-yet-passed.

Finishing Foundations waives the BCAB readiness assessment

Practitioners entering Stage 2 without the training take a readiness review first, at GHS 150. Holding the Foundations Certificate removes that step. It is the one concrete, priced advantage this certificate carries, and it is why finishing matters more than enrolling.
